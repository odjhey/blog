---
title: "You call the shots, not your tools"
date: "2024-04-18"
tags: ["programming", "application-design"]
description: "Our tools should do our bidding, not lead us into over-engineered, needlessly complex solutions."
---

I often see devs get enslaved by the tools they are using. Typed languages, for instance, yes we want IntelliSense, yes we want those compile/transpile time checks, but should these be the core driving reasons for introducing chunks of code in our codebases?

Seeing devs get led to implementing abstractions and features because the compiler suggests it often leads to [over-engineered, needlessly complex solutions](/blog/prefer-the-least-expressive/). I often catch myself in this position as well.

Yes, the compiler is hinting at something, but maybe it's not about writing workarounds and stuff. It may be a sign to step back, look at it at a higher level, and you may find the problem to be more about the structure of things rather than that specific line with red squigglies.

What we want is for these tools to do our bidding. We should be calling the shots, not the other way around.
