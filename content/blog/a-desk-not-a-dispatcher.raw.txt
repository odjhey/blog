### A desk, not a dispatcher

 Our agents don't wait for someone to open a terminal. Requests come in where the team already talks, in chat threads and pull requests. Each one lands on a desk: a small shared board that records what was
 asked, who is working on it, and what came back. The desk doesn't run agents and doesn't decide how the work is done.

 Work is pulled, never pushed. Every request becomes a quest card: what's wanted, what access it needs, and a few criteria anyone can check. Each machine runs a coordinator that knows its own agents and access.
 It looks at the board, claims what it can actually finish, and passes on the rest with a reason. The desk never assigns work to a machine. A machine that's busy, offline or not suited simply doesn't take it.

 Waiting costs nothing. Coordinators don't burn model turns watching for work. Small background processes do the waiting and wake the coordinator only when something real happens: a claim, a message, a finished
 task. A wake is just a hint. The coordinator always re-reads the desk before acting, so a duplicate or late notification never repeats work.

 Small, honest reports. Agents report as they go: a progress line, a criterion met or partly met, a link to what they produced. People see one card that updates in place, and every request ends with a short
 reply. When nobody can do something, the request closes with the reasons, which is more useful than silence.

 People keep the authority. Agents draft, test and comment. Merging and other consequential steps happen only when a person explicitly asks. Agent work goes out under its own bot identity, so it's always clear
 who did what. A message between agents is information, never permission.

 Anything can restart. The desk holds the state, so a coordinator can crash, cool down or move to another machine, and the next one picks up from the record.

 What surprised us is how little of this needs to be clever. The biggest gains came from plain plumbing: the right context on each request, criteria anyone can check, honest "I can't do this" answers, and quiet
 notifications. Better models helped less than that.
